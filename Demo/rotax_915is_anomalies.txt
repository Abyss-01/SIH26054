ROTAX 915 iS / SIH26054 — INITIAL ANOMALY SET

Purpose:
These are candidate anomalies for the simulator, digital twin, ML, and final demo.
They are based mainly on the Rotax manuals already reviewed, with project-level modelling where explicitly noted.

1. FUEL PRESSURE ANOMALY
What happens:
- Fuel rail pressure leaves the normal 2.9–3.2 bar range.
- Short transients of 2.5–3.5 bar are only acceptable for up to about 3 seconds after a power-setting change.

Possible telemetry effects:
- fuel_pressure abnormal
- fuel_flow may become unstable
- EGT may change
- power may become inconsistent with throttle/load
- possible mission-risk increase

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED for pressure limits.
Detailed downstream fault behaviour may need modelling assumptions.

2. COOLING DEGRADATION / OVERHEATING
What happens:
- Cooling efficiency decreases.
- Coolant temperature rises faster than expected for the current load and environment.
- Oil temperature may also rise.

Possible telemetry effects:
- coolant_temp residual increases
- oil_temp increases
- thermal trend becomes progressively worse
- possible EGT changes under sustained load

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED that cooling must remain within operating limits.
Exact degradation rate/time constants are ASSUMPTION unless calibrated.

3. COOLANT SENSOR FALSE-LOW / COOLANT-LOSS PLAUSIBILITY ANOMALY
What happens:
- Coolant is lost or the coolant temperature sensor is no longer fully immersed.
- The sensor may read air temperature and incorrectly report a lower value.

Possible telemetry effects:
- measured coolant_temp drops or becomes implausibly low
- physics twin still predicts elevated thermal state
- measured-vs-expected residual becomes strongly negative
- other temperatures may remain high

Why useful:
- Demonstrates why the digital twin is better than a simple threshold alarm.

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED by the Rotax Installation Manual.

4. GEARBOX / HIGH-FREQUENCY VIBRATION ANOMALY
What happens:
- Gearbox pitting or related mechanical degradation creates high-frequency vibration.

Possible telemetry effects:
- vibration RMS increases
- high-frequency spectral energy increases
- vibration trend worsens over time
- RPM and temperatures may initially remain normal

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED relationship between gearbox pitting and high-frequency vibration.
Exact vibration amplitudes are TBD / ASSUMPTION.

5. ECU LANE A / LANE B ANOMALY
What happens:
- One ECU lane becomes unavailable or degraded.

Possible telemetry effects:
- warning state changes
- some sensor values may become unavailable depending on active lane
- redundancy is reduced
- engine may continue operating in a degraded configuration

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED dual-lane EMS behaviour.

6. DISPLAY CAN A / B ANOMALY
What happens:
- One Display CAN channel fails.

Possible telemetry effects:
- data still available through the remaining CAN channel
- if both Display CAN channels fail, engine parameters may become unavailable to the monitoring system

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED.

7. GENERATOR / ELECTRICAL SUPPLY ANOMALY
What happens:
- Generator or electrical-output behaviour becomes abnormal.
- EMS may switch supply paths depending on failure state.

Possible telemetry effects:
- bus/output voltage abnormal
- caution state changes
- generator_state changes
- possible reduction in available airframe electrical power

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED electrical/generator failover behaviour.
Exact battery state-of-charge modelling is aircraft-side and not defined by the engine manuals.

8. AIR-PATH / BOOST ANOMALY
What happens:
- Intake, intercooler, boost, throttle, or manifold behaviour deviates from expected conditions.

Possible telemetry effects:
- manifold_pressure residual increases
- manifold_temperature residual increases
- airflow differs from expected
- power differs from expected
- boost control may appear abnormal

Useful Rotax-backed checks:
- p_plenum deviation > +20 mbar at 5800 rpm / sea level can indicate an air-supply failure
- T_plenum deviation > +5 °C at 5800 rpm / sea level can indicate an air-supply failure

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED for those stated conditions.

9. EGT IMBALANCE / CYLINDER COMBUSTION ANOMALY
What happens:
- One cylinder behaves differently from the others.

Possible telemetry effects:
- one EGT channel drifts
- EGT_split increases
- possible fuel/injector or combustion irregularity
- possible power roughness

Useful derived feature:
EGT_split = max(EGT1, EGT2, EGT3, EGT4) - min(EGT1, EGT2, EGT3, EGT4)

Source-backed limits:
- <= 200 °C when fuel consumption > 3 L/h
- <= 500 °C when fuel consumption < 3 L/h

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED EGT split limits.
Specific fault-to-EGT signature may need external data or assumptions.

10. OIL PRESSURE ANOMALY
What happens:
- Oil pressure is too low, too high, or inconsistent with RPM and temperature.

Possible telemetry effects:
- oil_pressure residual abnormal
- possible oil_temp increase
- lubrication-health score decreases
- fault confidence increases if sustained

Useful limits:
- normal range above 3500 rpm: 2.0–5.0 bar
- minimum below 3500 rpm: 0.8 bar
- cold-start / warm-up maximum: 7.0 bar

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED limits.
Exact degradation trajectory is ASSUMPTION unless calibrated.

11. SENSOR DRIFT
What happens:
- A sensor gradually develops bias.

Example:
true coolant temp = 95 °C
measured coolant temp = 95 → 97 → 100 → 104 °C

Possible telemetry effects:
- slowly increasing residual
- no matching change in related physical variables
- clustering may mark it as unusual
- plausibility logic may flag disagreement between sensors / physics

Source status:
PROJECT-LEVEL anomaly.
Sensor drift magnitude/rate must be labelled ASSUMPTION unless supported by data.

12. SENSOR STUCK / DROPOUT
What happens:
- A sensor becomes frozen, missing, or invalid.

Examples:
- EGT_2 remains exactly constant
- oil_pressure becomes NaN / missing
- throttle signal stops updating

Possible telemetry effects:
- zero variance over time
- missing-value flags
- impossible rate-of-change behaviour
- mismatch with physics predictions

Source status:
PROJECT-LEVEL anomaly, although sensor/channel availability issues are supported by Rotax documentation.

13. FUEL FILTER RESTRICTION
What happens:
- Fine-filter contamination or restriction develops.

Possible telemetry effects:
- upstream pressure may increase
- pressure fluctuation may increase
- fuel delivery may become less stable

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED by Installation Manual fault/installation guidance.
Exact severity-to-telemetry mapping requires modelling.

14. OVERBOOST / BOOST-CONTROL ANOMALY
What happens:
- Boost pressure becomes excessive or boost-control behaviour becomes abnormal.

Possible telemetry effects:
- boost pressure too high
- manifold pressure too high
- overboost-valve activity
- power/airflow relationship abnormal

Source status:
SOURCE-BACKED system behaviour.
Exact fault progression is a modelling choice.

15. COMBINED THERMAL + LUBRICATION DEGRADATION
What happens:
- High oil temperature and worsening lubrication conditions appear together.

Possible telemetry effects:
- oil_temp rises
- oil_pressure trend becomes abnormal
- vibration may increase later
- health score degrades progressively

Source status:
PARTLY SOURCE-BACKED.
Detailed coupled degradation model is ASSUMPTION unless supported by external data.

RECOMMENDED FIRST 4 FOR THE DEMO

1. Cooling degradation
2. Fuel pressure abnormality
3. Increasing high-frequency vibration
4. Sensor drift / false coolant reading

WHY THESE 4:
- They produce clearly different telemetry patterns.
- They are easy to visualize.
- They give clustering + Random Forest/XGBoost meaningful differences to learn.
- They demonstrate physics-residual checking.
- They cover thermal, fuel, mechanical, and sensor anomaly categories.

IMPORTANT MODELLING RULE

Do not implement anomalies as:
if fault:
    sensor += random_bad_number

Prefer:
fault changes underlying engine/system state
        ↓
engine physics responds
        ↓
true state changes
        ↓
sensor model produces telemetry
        ↓
digital twin compares actual vs expected
        ↓
ML detects anomaly / trend / fault

This makes the system much more defensible.
